TEAMPULSE - USER GUIDE (HOW TO ACTUALLY USE THE APP)
================================================================================
Last updated: 18 November 2026
For: anyone opening the app for the first time - demo viewer, friend, or
interviewer. No technical knowledge assumed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT THIS APP IS FOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TeamPulse is a place for a small team to keep its work in one spot instead of
scattering it across chat and spreadsheets.

You can:
  - make a team and invite people,
  - group work into projects,
  - put tasks on a Kanban board and move them along,
  - comment on tasks and see what changed,
  - ask AI to score a task's priority and summarise your day.

4. FIRST FIVE MINUTES - A GUIDED TOUR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 1  Log in as arjun@teampulse.ac.
Step 2  Open Dashboard. You see total projects, total tasks, status counts, a
        list of recent activity, a velocity chart, a workload chart, and a
        "Today's Standup" card.
        - The two charts are fixed sample data, not live numbers. Treat them as
          placeholders.
        - The standup card currently fails because of a backend bug. See
          section 9.
Step 3  Go to Projects. Open "Web Platform" (key WEB).
Step 4  Click the board.
Step 5  Drag a card from Backlog to In Progress. The move is saved: refresh the
        page and it stays where you put it.
Step 6  Click a card. The task detail page opens.
Step 7  Write a comment. It appears in the list and a row is added to the
        activity log.
Step 8  Press "AI Priority" (or the score box). You get a 1-10 score, a short
        reason, and an action such as "Do now" or "Defer".
Step 9  Go to Team. You see the four members and their roles.
Step 10 Go to Settings and switch the theme. The choice sticks after refresh
        because it is stored in the browser.

That tour touches every major feature. Ten minutes, and you have seen the whole
product.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. TEAMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 Create a team
    Give it a name. You become the owner automatically. A user can only own or
    join the teams they are added to; there is no public team directory.

5.2 Invite someone
    Enter the email of a person who has ALREADY registered. There is no email
    invitation flow - the backend looks up the existing account and adds it. If
    the email is unknown, you get an error, not a pending invite.

5.3 Roles and what they mean
    owner   full control, including deleting the team and changing anyone's role
    admin   manage members and work, cannot remove the owner
    member  create and edit tasks, comment
    viewer  read-only

    Rules you will actually notice:
      - You cannot demote the last owner.
      - A member cannot promote themselves.
      - Only the author can edit their own comment; author or admin can delete.

5.4 Remove a member
    From the team page, if your role allows it. Their tasks stay in the project
    but become unassigned.

    Known gap: the "Save" and "Delete team" buttons on the team settings screen
    are not connected to the API yet.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. PROJECTS AND TASKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 Create a project
    Name it and give it a short key (2-5 uppercase letters), for example WEB or
    MOB. The key is used as a human-friendly prefix for the work.

6.2 Create a task
    Required: title. Optional but useful: description, status, priority,
    assignee, story points, due date.

    Where it lands: the column you choose (default Backlog).

    Known gap: the assignee dropdown in the create/edit dialog can be empty
    because the board does not pass the member list into the dialog. If that
    happens, create the task first, then assign it from the task detail page.

6.3 The five columns
    Backlog      not planned yet
    To Do        planned for now
    In Progress  someone is working on it
    Review       waiting for a look
    Done         finished

6.4 Moving work
    Drag a card to another column. Both the status and the position inside the
    column are saved. Two people dragging at the same time can produce a
    surprising order; refresh to see the stored state.

6.5 Editing and deleting
    Open the card, use the edit action. Deleting removes the task and writes a
    "deleted" row into the activity log.

6.6 Task detail page
    Left: title, description, status, priority, assignee, due date, story points.
    Right/below: comments, activity timeline, AI priority panel.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. COMMENTS AND ACTIVITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Comments    plain text, newest at the bottom. Edit within your own comment,
              delete if you are the author or an admin.
  Activity    an audit trail: created, updated, moved, assigned, commented,
              deleted. Each row stores the old and new value, so "moved from
              To Do to In Progress" is real data, not a guess.

  Important expectation setting: activity does NOT update live in another
  browser tab right now. The WebSocket connection opens and presence works, but
  task and comment changes are not broadcast. Refresh to see another user's
  edits.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. THE AI FEATURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.1 Task priority
    Open a task and request the score. You get:
      score     1 (low) to 10 (urgent)
      reason    a sentence explaining the number
      action    Do now / Schedule this week / Delegate / Defer

    What it looks at: title, description, priority, due date, current status,
    and how overdue it is.
    Where the number comes from: the AI microservice, which calls Groq
    (Llama 3.3 70B). If the key is missing or the call fails, a local rule-based
    fallback produces the score instead. So the feature never hard-fails.
    What it does not do: save the score. The field stays empty in the database,
    so refreshing loses the displayed value.

8.2 Standup summary
    The dashboard card is meant to show "what I did, what I will do, blockers"
    plus three priorities. Currently it errors - see section 9.

8.3 Cost and privacy note
    Prompts include your task titles and descriptions. On a shared deployment,
    do not put anything confidential in a task.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. KNOWN ISSUES YOU WILL SEE (AND SHOULD NOT PANIC ABOUT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. "Today's Standup" card shows an error.
     Cause: the endpoint expects a date as a query parameter while the frontend
     sends it in the body, plus a bad import on the server. It is a real bug
     with a known fix.

  2. Dashboard charts never change.
     They are hard-coded sample data. Every account sees the same five sprint
     rows and three people.

  3. Notification toggles do not do anything outside the browser.
     They are saved to localStorage. No email or push is sent.

  4. Assignee list is empty when creating a task.
     The board does not pass members into the dialog.

  5. Two browser tabs do not sync in real time.
     No broadcast is emitted on task/comment changes.

  6. "Delete account" and "Delete team" buttons appear to do nothing.
     They are not wired to the API.

  7. Forgot password says "email sent" but nothing arrives.
     The endpoint exists; no mail provider is configured.

  8. Project page links to /analytics and a team settings page that 404.
     Those routes were planned but not built.

  None of these break the core loop: create project -> create task -> move it ->
  comment -> score it. That loop works.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. TIPS FOR DEMOING THIS APP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Start with the board, not the dashboard. The board is the strongest part.
  - Drag a card, refresh, and show that it persisted. That single move proves
    the API, the database and the optimistic UI all work together.
  - Then open a task and run the AI score twice with different due dates. Show
    that the number changes with context.
  - Mention the gaps before the interviewer finds them. "Real-time broadcast is
    stubbed; presence works, task sync is the next fix" is a much better answer
    than being caught.
  - Have the Swagger page open in another tab (http://localhost:8000/docs). It
    proves the API is real and documented.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. GLOSSARY FOR NON-DEVELOPERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Kanban board    columns of cards where moving a card = changing status
  Task            one unit of work
  Project         a group of tasks, inside a team
  Team            the group of people who share projects
  Role            what you are allowed to do
  Activity log    automatic history of changes
  AI priority     a score from 1-10 suggesting urgency
  Standup         a short daily summary of work
  Token/session   the thing that keeps you logged in
